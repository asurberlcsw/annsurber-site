About My Approach

I provide individual psychodynamic psychotherapy to adults (18+) in Oregon and Illinois.

This way of working is grounded in the idea that we don't always know why we act and feel the way we do, and that much of emotional life occurs out of awareness. Our ways of being are informed by past experiences, as well as current environments and context.

Psychodynamic therapy explores how these influences shape your inner experience and sense of self. By looking closely at the unique constellation of your life, we have an opportunity to learn about how your mind works, how you’ve come to be who you are, and how you relate to yourself and others.

Patterns of relating to others, and the symptoms that bring you to therapy, can often be meaningful expressions of your life’s story, ways you’ve survived and adapted to the world around you. In therapy, I hope to help you make sense of your story by offering a relationship within which you can tell it. As you grow in your awareness of yourself, new ways of feeling, living, and relating can become possible.

You can expect a confidential, collaborative space to show up as yourself and say what is on your mind. Whether it’s a flicker of a painful memory, an onslaught of self-deprecations and “shoulds”, a knot in your stomach, a recurring conflict, a longing for connection, the weight of grief, not knowing what to do, not doing what you want, an exciting dream or fantasy, a seed inside you that is growing, or perhaps feeling outside of yourself or absolutely nothing at all, all of it belongs here. This is a space to look together and become curious about the experience of your mind, body, and heart, no matter where you're starting from. I'll listen deeply, engage thoughtfully, notice with you what may be hard to see on your own, and help you get in touch with yourself.

Your feelings toward me are part of the process, as patterns that occur outside of therapy are likely to emerge in our relationship. This can lead to meaningful insight and change as we notice and understand these patterns together in real time.

There is no right way to do therapy, and the direction we go will ultimately be unique to you (and us). My style is warm, with a touch of humor and frankness. It is important to me to create an atmosphere where you feel safe and respected, and we will move at a pace that feels right for you.

Who I Work With

People seek therapy for many reasons. Some concerns I work with include:

  • anxiety and depression (persistent worry, self-criticism, low mood, burnout, feeling stuck)
  • relational and complex trauma (childhood experiences, family dynamics, sexual trauma)
  • self-esteem (perfectionism, shame, imposter syndrome, difficulty trusting yourself)
  • body image and relationship with food (body dissatisfaction, binge eating, emotional eating)
  • grief and loss (bereavement, endings, breakups)
  • identity (race, gender, sexuality, familial roles, cultural belonging, questioning and exploration)
  • life transitions (moving, school or career change, pregnancy and postpartum, illness, caregiving)
  • relationship difficulties (dating, conflict, communication, infidelity, intimacy, boundaries)
  • loneliness (difficulty finding community, feeling misunderstood, social anxiety, isolation)
  • or a persistent sense of disconnection, dissatisfaction, or emptiness (feeling numb, lacking purpose, wondering if life could feel different)
Maybe something just feels off or hard to put words to. We can talk about that here.

While I treat people from all walks of life, I often work with young adults who have experienced early trauma, who are now coping with the pressures and uncertainties of adulthood and relationships. I have specialized expertise working with BIPOC and LGBTQ+ clients. I consider myself particularly attuned to the experiences of those who may feel outside of dominant norms and expectations, or feel caught between different worlds, relationships, or versions of themselves.
